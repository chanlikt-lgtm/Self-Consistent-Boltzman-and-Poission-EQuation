\section*{2. Grid-to-grid behaviour --- NON-MONOTONE}
Refining $h_{\rm rep}$ by $\sqrt2$ per step (ratios $1.412,\,1.420$), the drain peak $\Gii$ does
\emph{not} move in one direction:
\[
\underbrace{3.0756}_{71\times58}\;\xrightarrow{\;-30.5\%\;}\;\underbrace{2.1362}_{100\times70}\;
\xrightarrow{\;+11.8\%\;}\;\underbrace{2.3874}_{141\times85}\quad[\ten{25}\ \text{cm}^{-3}\text{s}^{-1}].
\]
The sequence reverses direction (down, then up). $G_{\rm tot}$ behaves the same
($9.265\!\to\!6.025\!\to\!6.526\ten{13}$; $-35\%$ then $+8.3\%$). $T_e^{\max}$ is essentially flat
($3184/3161/3194$\,K, $\pm\sim20$\,K). \textbf{This reversal is the central fact of the audit.}

\section*{3. Is Richardson extrapolation even valid here?}
\textbf{Geometry check (passes):} $r_x=1.414$, $r_y=1.410/1.425$, both within 5\% of $\sqrt2$, so a
single representative $h\equiv h_{\rm rep}$ is defensible (spacings taken from the \emph{physical}
mesh, not from $N_x$).

\textbf{Asymptotic-model check (fails):} classical Richardson assumes a one-term regime
$Q(h)=Q^\star + C\,h^p+\cdots$, for which the observed order follows from
\[
r^{\,p}=\frac{Q_1-Q_2}{Q_2-Q_3}=\frac{+9.40\ten{24}}{-2.51\ten{24}}=-3.74 .
\]
A \emph{negative} ratio has \textbf{no real solution $p$} ($r^p>0$ for every real $p$): the three
nodes are not describable by a single-term power law. The gated fit flags the reversal as
\emph{genuine}, not solver noise:
\[
S_{\rm rev}(\Gii)=\frac{|Q_2-Q_3|}{u_{23}}=\frac{2.51\ten{24}}{3.41\ten{21}}=737\gg1,
\qquad S_{\rm rev}(G_{\rm tot})=524\gg1,
\]
where $u_{23}\!\sim\!3\ten{21}$ is the per-node plateau/solver uncertainty (five orders of magnitude
below the reversal). \textbf{Three-node Richardson extrapolation is therefore not justified for
$\Gii$ (nor $G_{\rm tot}$) on this mesh family, and the harness suppresses it.}

\section*{4. Extrapolated quantities: NOT reported}
Because the asymptotic-model check fails, we deliberately do \emph{not} report an observed order
$p_{\rm obs}$, a continuum $\Gii^{\infty}$, or a GCI/discretisation-error band --- manufacturing an
extrapolated value from a sign-reversing sequence would be numerically meaningless. The correct
audit result is the \emph{absence} of a justified extrapolation. The tested meshes bound
$\Gii^{\max}\in[2.14,\,3.08]\ten{25}$ with finest-grid value $2.39\ten{25}$; no continuum value is
claimed. A rigorous continuum estimate would require a second mesh family (fixed $y$-mesh, refine
drain-$x$) to disentangle the anisotropic $x$/$y$ error and re-test for a consistent $p$.

\section*{5. Scientific verdict --- two separate questions}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item[\textbf{(a)}] \textbf{Absolute $\Gii$ convergence: NOT demonstrably asymptotic.} The three
drain-refined nodes do not occupy a single asymptotic regime; no continuum peak $\Gii$ can be
rigorously quoted. The absolute drain-avalanche magnitude remains grid-limited.
\item[\textbf{(b)}] \textbf{Qualitative / physical conclusions: ROBUST.} The non-monotone reversal
itself is a robust finding ($S_{\rm rev}\gg1$, far above noise), not a numerical artefact. The
broader reproduced physics is likewise robust and independent of this extrapolation gap:
inversion/transport ($n^{\max}\!\approx\!1.0\ten{20}$, $|v|\!\approx\!1\ten{7}$\,cm/s,
$T_e^{\max}\!\approx\!3180$\,K), drain-localised avalanche, and the $\sim$37\% self-consistent
$\Gii$-feedback suppression established across the coupled runs.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{These must not be conflated:} the absolute avalanche magnitude is not asymptotically
converged, \emph{yet} the qualitative picture (drain-localised, self-consistently suppressed,
non-monotone under refinement) stands. That is the honest, and scientifically sufficient, closeout
of the reproduction.
